A full awards season turns the whole run of film awards into one game. You draft a roster of contenders once, and that roster scores at every event the season counts, from the first nominations announcement to the last winners. This page explains how one works. For the basics of leagues, ceremonies and drafting, see [how it works](/how-it-works).

## What a full awards season is

A single-ceremony season is built around one awards show: you draft from that ceremony's nominees, and the game is over when the winners are announced.

A full awards season is built around the whole run of events. You draft once, before the season starts, and your roster keeps scoring as each event announces its nominations and its winners, in the order the events happen. Because the nominations come out before anyone knows who will win, you pick contenders rather than nominees: a film, or a film and a person for acting categories. Any film counts, nominated yet or not.

Between events you can change your roster with free agency and waiver claims, so the game stays alive all season.

## Setting one up

Where full awards seasons are available to your league, the commissioner starts one from Create season by choosing Full awards season. Players join the season from its page, and the commissioner locks it when everyone is in. The season shows a short summary of each area of settings, and each summary opens in a window with the detail:

- **Events.** Which events count, and how much each is worth. A group of ceremonies counts as one event, so it is turned on or off as a whole. An event's weight multiplies the points it awards.
- **Scoring.** Points per nomination and per win. By default that is 1 for a nomination and 2 more for a win. Each category can also have its own weight.
- **Roster.** How many contenders each player holds (16 by default) and, if the commissioner wants, a limit on how many can come from one category.
- **Draft.** The draft is always a snake draft. The commissioner sets the pick clock (90 seconds by default, or no clock at all) and whether auto-draft is on.

Everything can be changed until the commissioner locks the season. After that the roster, scoring, events and member list are frozen, and only the pick clock and auto-draft can still change. Nothing changes once the draft starts. Anyone in the league can read the settings in the Rules page of the season.

Some full awards seasons are marked Coming soon. You can set one up and invite players early, but the draft can only start once the season goes live.

## The draft

The commissioner starts the draft. Seats are assigned at random, and the draft is a snake: the order runs forward in the first round, backward in the second, and so on, until every roster is full.

When it is your turn, pick a contender. If you cannot be there, use your queue. You can rank contenders in your queue ahead of time, and you can edit it at any point, including during the draft. If your pick clock runs out and auto-draft is on, the system takes the first contender in your queue that is still available and fits your roster. It never picks anyone who is not in your queue. You can also stage contenders: staged contenders are ones you want to draft by hand, and auto-draft never takes them.

If a pick is missed, because the clock ran out and nothing in the queue could be taken, or because auto-draft is off, that pick is not lost. After the regular rounds, each missed pick gets one supplemental pick, in the order the original picks were missed. If you miss a supplemental pick too, you get a priority pass for the original pick instead. See the waivers below for what a priority pass does.

## During the season

Once the draft is complete, the season is in progress. You can see your roster, and anyone else's, with each pick's points so far. The transactions log lists every roster change in the season.

**Free agency.** Any contender nobody owns is a free agent. You can add one whenever no waiver window is open, as long as you have room on your roster and the category has room. If your roster is full, you pick someone to drop in the same move. You can drop any pick at any time.

**Waiver windows.** When a scoring day arrives, free agency closes and a waiver window opens, so nobody can grab a free agent the moment the news breaks. Windows are set by the events' dates and are shown on the waivers page. A window covers the scoring day and the two days after it. If another scoring day falls inside an open window, the window stretches to cover it, and consecutive scoring days share one window. Free agency reopens at midnight Pacific after the window's last day. Dropping is always allowed.

While a window is open you file waiver claims instead. A claim names the contender you want. It can also name a pick to drop, which happens only if the claim wins. You rank your claims, and you can reorder, edit or withdraw them until the window ends.

When the window ends the claims run, all together:

1. Claims with a priority pass go first, earliest missed pick first.
2. The rest go in waiver priority order. Priority starts as the reverse of the draft order, so the last pick of the draft is first in line.
3. Each player's own claims run in the order they ranked them.

A claim fails if someone already owns the contender or if it does not fit your roster or category limits at that point. A priority pass is used up only by a claim that wins. A claim that loses keeps it. Anyone who wins a claim moves to the bottom of the priority list once, no matter how many they won. The results of each run are visible to the whole league. All priority passes expire when the first window's claims have run.

## Scoring

Every event has two scoring days: the day nominations are announced and the day winners are announced. For each, every contender that was nominated, or won, scores for one player: the one who owned it at midnight Pacific at the start of that day. Nominations and wins announced on the same day share that moment.

So what counts is who holds a contender at that moment, not who held it later or earlier. Picks you add after midnight do not score for that day, and dropping someone later in the day does not take back the points. If you drop a contender between its nomination and its win, you keep the nomination points and whoever picks it up gets the win. A contender that scores once in a day scores once, even if it is nominated in more than one category of an event.

Points are the base for a nomination or a win, multiplied by the category's weight and the event's weight. A nominated winner that you held throughout earns both, 3 points with the default settings. Events the commissioner left out do not count.

Standings add up every player's points across the season. They list the players from the highest score down, and tied players share a rank. Each event shows which picks scored and why, so you can see where every point came from. If the draft finishes on or after an event's scoring day, nobody scores for that day, since nobody owned anything at midnight.

## The end

The season is complete when every event it counts has announced its winners and those points are in. The standings then add Final results, which shows every contender that scored and how much each earned, or every player's final roster. The player with the most points wins.
